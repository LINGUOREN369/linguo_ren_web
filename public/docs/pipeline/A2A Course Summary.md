# A2A Course Summary — Accelerated Algebra 2 (2026–27)

> The top of the planning stack: **course goals → units → days.** Read this before building or
> teaching any unit. Unit detail lives in each unit summary (A2A Unit 2 Summary,
> A2A Unit 3 Summary); pacing lives in A2A Planning Sheet.

---

## Course goals

By the end of the course, students can:

1. **Manipulate and solve equations fluently and strategically**, and know when an answer is valid.
2. **Treat functions as objects** to read, combine, and invert across graphs, equations, and tables.
3. **Recognize the major families of functions** and how they behave.
4. **Use them to model and reason** about real situations.

**Where it leads:** Precalculus picks up with polynomial and rational functions, then composition,
inverses, and transformations, then Calculus.

## The course in one line

Solving becomes a choice, not a procedure, and functions become objects: students learn to pick a
method from an equation's structure, then to read, transform, and build the function families they
will need in Precalculus.

## Threads across units

These are the connections worth naming out loud when the earlier end comes up:

- **"No real solution" → complex numbers.** Unit 2 ends some equations at "no real solution";
  Unit 5 gives those same equations complex solutions.
- **Reading one function → moving families.** Unit 3 reads a single function; Unit 4 transforms
  whole families of them.
- **Extraneous roots.** Introduced in Unit 1's rational and radical equations, back on Unit 2 Day 5,
  and again in Unit 3's spiral review.
- **Systems.** Unit 2 Day 4 solves a system with a quadratic; Unit 3 Day 4 counts a system's
  solutions without solving.
- **Quadratics → polynomials → Precalculus.** Units 2 and 5 grow into Unit 6, which is where
  Precalculus starts.

## How this is used day to day

- **Building a unit:** its unit summary names which course goals the unit serves and which threads
  it carries in from earlier units or sets up for later ones. After the unit is built, update its
  row above.
- **Opening Questions** revisit the previous class's core topic, so each day starts by retrieving
  the last one (fluency).
- **Practice sheets** repeat the same skills across 8 sets with new numbers (fluency through
  varied repetition).
- **Closing Questions** are big-idea questions answered in words, not by computing (connections,
  not procedures).
- **When a thread's earlier end comes up,** say where it goes next, so students see the course as
  one story instead of a list of topics.
